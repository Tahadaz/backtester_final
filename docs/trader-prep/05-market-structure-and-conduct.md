# Market Structure & Conduct

What the syllabus math doesn't teach: how each of your three markets actually
works and how participants are expected to behave. Read across weeks 1–6,
revisit before every interview. Interviewers use this material to separate
"studied finance" from "understands the desk."

## 1. The eurobond market (ICMA's world)

- **What a eurobond is:** a bond issued outside the jurisdiction of its
  currency (a Moroccan sovereign USD bond sold to international investors is a
  eurobond; nothing to do with EUR the currency). OTC market, self-regulated
  largely by **ICMA** rules; settled T+2 through **Euroclear/Clearstream**;
  conventions: typically annual coupons, 30E/360 or ACT/ACT (ICMA), clean-price
  quotation.
- **Primary market:** issuer mandates a syndicate of banks → announcement, IPTs
  (initial price thoughts) → book-building → guidance tightens → final spread
  vs benchmark (UST for USD, mid-swaps for EUR) → allocation. Know the terms:
  NIP (new-issue premium), book coverage, will-not-grow. Juniors watch the new
  issue pipeline daily — start noting big EM sovereign prints in the blotter.
- **Secondary market:** dealer market, request-for-quote (RFQ) electronic (MarketAxess,
  Bloomberg) + voice for size; liquidity is episodic, positions are hedged with
  govvies/futures → a credit trader's P&L = spread P&L + hedge slippage.
- **Spread measures** (be fluent): G-spread (vs interpolated govt), I-spread
  (vs swaps), Z-spread (parallel shift over the zero curve), ASW. And the EM
  sovereign toolkit: ratings trajectory, external debt profile, FX reserves,
  IMF programs. **Case study: Morocco** — investment-grade-cusp sovereign,
  USD/EUR curve outstanding; know its rating, roughly where its 10y trades vs
  UST, and its 2026 flexibilization context. This is your differentiator.

## 2. The FX market (the Code's world)

- **Structure:** ~$7.5tn/day (BIS Triennial 2022); decentralized OTC; spot,
  forwards, **FX swaps (the biggest segment — funding, not speculation)**,
  NDFs, options. Tiered liquidity: interdealer (EBS/Refinitiv primary venues) →
  single-dealer platforms/aggregators → clients. Prime brokerage lets funds
  trade in a bank's name.
- **Roles:** market-maker (quotes two-way, manages inventory/skew) vs taker;
  internalization (banks match client flow before hedging externally);
  algo execution vs risk transfer. Know what "the desk warehouses risk" means.
- **Settlement:** T+2 mostly, PvP via **CLS** kills Herstatt risk for member
  currencies; MAD is not CLS-settled — settlement risk is live in your corner
  of the world (and a Dec-2024 Code update theme).
- **Fixes:** WMR 4pm London fix, the 2013 fixing scandal → why the Code exists.

## 3. Commodities futures (the CME/ICE world)

- **Exchange-traded, centrally cleared** — unlike your other two markets. Know:
  initial/variation margin, daily settlement, position limits, physical vs cash
  settlement (WTI physical Cushing delivery vs Brent cash-settled vs gold
  Comex delivery).
- **The curve is the market:** contango/backwardation, storage and convenience
  yield, roll costs for index products (USO in 2020 negative-WTI as the
  classic case study — be able to tell the April 2020 story end to end).
- **Participants:** producers/consumers hedging, merchants running basis,
  CTAs/managed futures (your TSM literature), index money. Commitment of
  Traders report exists (CFTC) — glance monthly.
- **Benchmarks:** WTI vs Brent (grades, delivery points, Brent-WTI spread
  drivers), gold vs real yields + DXY (your blotter already tracks this).

## 4. Conduct — the FX Global Code (and its spirit everywhere)

Read the actual Code (globalfxc.org, Dec 2024, 55 principles). Minimum
fluency for interviews:

- **The six leading principles areas:** ethics, governance, execution,
  information sharing, risk management & compliance, confirmation/settlement.
- **Last look (Principle 17):** a liquidity provider's final check before
  accepting a trade; controversial because of asymmetric rejection and what may
  be done with the information during the window. Know the accepted practice:
  no trading on last-look information, symmetric application, disclosure.
- **Pre-hedging:** permitted only when acting as principal, in a manner meant
  to benefit the client, with disclosure — GFXC published dedicated guidance;
  be able to give a clean example of acceptable vs front-running.
- **Information handling:** no sharing of client identities/flows ("mark-ups
  and axes yes, names no"); what counts as market colour vs confidential flow.
- **Dec-2024 update themes:** FX settlement-risk principles strengthened; more
  transparency on client-generated data use on e-platforms.
- Equivalent spirit elsewhere: ICMA primary-market handbook conventions
  (allocations, stabilization) for bonds; exchange rules + CFTC/position-limit
  regime for futures. One paragraph of fluency on each is enough.
